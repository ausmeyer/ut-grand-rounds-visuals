ILINet

U.S. Outpatient Influenza-like Illness Surveillance Network

Reports: The weekly percentage of outpatient visits meeting the influenza-like illness definition, reported by age group.

How to read it: Use it to follow how much outpatient care is for flu-like illness. Other respiratory viruses can cause the same symptoms, so this is not a count of confirmed influenza.

0:30

NSSP

National Syndromic Surveillance Program

Reports: The percentage of emergency-department visits with an influenza discharge diagnosis, overall and by age group.

How to read it: Use it to follow how much emergency care involves a flu diagnosis. Testing and coding practices affect the signal; a diagnosis does not always mean a positive test.

CDC source ↗
0:50

FluSurv-NET

Influenza Hospitalization Surveillance Network

Reports: Population-based weekly rates and detailed characteristics of laboratory-confirmed influenza hospitalizations in selected catchment areas.

How to read it: Use it to compare hospitalization rates across ages and seasons and identify who is most affected. Rates describe monitored communities, not every U.S. hospital.

CDC source ↗
0:58

NHSN

National Healthcare Safety Network Hospital Respiratory Data Module

Reports: Weekly facility-level counts of new laboratory-confirmed influenza admissions, current hospitalizations, ICU occupancy, and bed capacity.

How to read it: Use it to track admissions and pressure on hospital and ICU capacity. These are hospital totals, not detailed records of individual patients.

CDC source ↗
1:14

ICLS

U.S. Influenza Collaborating Laboratories System

Reports: Weekly influenza tests and positive results. Public-health laboratory reports include virus types, subtypes or lineages, and patient ages when available.

How to read it: Use clinical-lab positivity to follow flu activity and public-health results to identify circulating viruses. Public-health samples often arrive already positive, so their positivity is not a community infection rate.

CDC source ↗
1:22

NREVSS

National Respiratory and Enteric Virus Surveillance System

Reports: Weekly test totals and positive results for influenza and other respiratory and enteric viruses, from hospital, commercial, and public-health laboratories.

How to read it: Use clinical-lab positivity to follow when and where flu is circulating. It describes tested specimens, not the percentage of all people infected.

CDC source ↗
1:44

NCHS

National Center for Health Statistics Mortality Surveillance

Reports: Weekly death-certificate measures for deaths coded with influenza, pneumonia, and/or COVID-19.

How to read it: Use influenza-coded deaths to follow flu's fatal impact over the season. Recent weeks are incomplete, and death certificates miss some deaths caused by flu.

CDC source ↗
1:52

Pediatric deaths

Influenza-Associated Pediatric Mortality Surveillance System

Reports: Individual laboratory-confirmed influenza-associated deaths among people younger than 18 years, with demographic and clinical details.

How to read it: Use it to track reported flu deaths in children and identify their clinical patterns. Recent counts can increase as additional reports arrive.
