System descriptions and sources
0:10

ILINet

U.S. Outpatient Influenza-like Illness Surveillance Network

What it measures: Weekly percentage of outpatient visits for influenza-like illness.

What it shows: When flu-like illness rises, peaks, and falls in the community.

CDC source ↗
0:32

NSSP

National Syndromic Surveillance Program

What it measures: Weekly percentage of emergency visits diagnosed as influenza.

What it shows: Flu trends in emergency care, nationally and by state.

CDC source ↗
0:52

FluSurv-NET

Influenza Hospitalization Surveillance Network

What it measures: Weekly lab-confirmed flu hospitalization rates in 90+ counties across 14 states.

What it shows: Who is hospitalized: rates by age, plus conditions, ICU use, and outcomes.

CDC source ↗
1:00

NHSN

National Healthcare Safety Network Hospital Respiratory Data (HRD)

What it measures: Weekly lab-confirmed flu admissions and inpatients, plus bed and ICU occupancy.

What it shows: Flu’s strain on hospital beds and ICUs, nationally and by state.

CDC source ↗
1:16

ICLS

U.S. Influenza Collaborating Laboratories System

What it measures: Weekly flu tests and positives; public health labs add subtype and lineage.

What it shows: The timing and intensity of flu activity, and which flu viruses predominate.

CDC source ↗
1:24

NREVSS

National Respiratory and Enteric Virus Surveillance System

What it measures: Weekly tests and positives for flu and other respiratory and enteric viruses.

What it shows: Seasonal patterns of RSV, COVID-19, and other viruses alongside flu.

CDC source ↗
1:46

NCHS

National Center for Health Statistics Mortality Surveillance

What it measures: Weekly percentage of deaths with flu listed on the death certificate.

What it shows: The rise and fall of flu deaths across the season, nationally and by state.

CDC source ↗
1:58

Pediatric deaths

Influenza-Associated Pediatric Mortality Surveillance System

What it measures: Individual reports of lab-confirmed flu deaths in children under 18.

What it shows: Each child’s age, vaccination status, and underlying conditions.

CDC source ↗
